# Content strategy

## The problem

The legacy site is thin. Eight service descriptions, two About paragraphs, contact
details. Nothing else — no proof, no process, no philosophy, no reason to believe.

A site built only from that content would be beautiful and empty.

## The client's reference

The client sent a competitor's article as a reference for the *kind* of content he
wants:

> Definitive Electronics — "Building a New Home in Florida? Bring a Home Technology
> Integrator in Early!"
> https://definitiveelectronics.com/blog/smart-home-planning-for-new-custom-homes/

~2,240 words. A single thesis, argued properly: **involve the technology integrator
during design, not mid-construction.**

Its argument, in order:
1. Technology is infrastructure now, like plumbing
2. Mid-construction changes are expensive
3. Collaborating with architect and designer produces better spaces
   (theater, lighting/shading, outdoor living)
4. Florida homes have specific demands — heat, humidity, storms
5. Everything depends on the network
6. Open walls are the moment to future-proof
7. The goal is simplicity

## What we take, and what we do not

**Take:** the thesis, the depth, the register. The idea that an integrator is a design
partner rather than an installer is an industry argument — it belongs to no one.

**Do not take:** their sentences. Copying is a copyright problem, and duplicate content
earns no search traffic — which defeats the purpose. Every line is written fresh.

**Do not exceed reality.** No invented services, project counts, awards, testimonials
or certifications. The page gets its substance from better argument, not from claims
Husky cannot support.

## The decision — two pages, in this order

### 1. Home — the main landing page (build first)

The company page. Carries the legacy content, restructured and given real depth:

- Hero — from "Smart Homes / When technology knocks on your door"
- Services — the 8 real services, organised and weighted rather than a flat grid
- About — the 20+ years, South Florida, the brand partners
- Contact — WhatsApp primary, plus phone, email, address

The upgrade over the legacy site is **structure and copy depth**, informed by the
reference article's register. The facts stay exactly what they are.

### 2. Philosophy page (build second)

A dedicated page carrying the "bring the integrator in early" thesis, written from
scratch for Husky.

This is **not** an About Us. About Us is about the company. This is about a point of
view — how a smart home should be planned, and why timing decides the outcome.

Working titles: *What We Believe* · *Our Philosophy* · *Our Approach*.
Route: `/philosophy` or `/new-construction` — decide at build time.

Its audience is different from the home page's: someone currently building or
renovating, at the moment the decision is live. That is why it earns its own page and
its own URL rather than a section.

Sections from the reference thesis that map cleanly onto Husky's real services:

| Thesis section | Husky service it grounds in |
|---|---|
| Plan before building | — (the argument itself) |
| Technology as infrastructure | Wi-Fi, Automation |
| Avoid mid-construction changes | Automation, Smart Lighting |
| Home theater design | Home Cinema |
| Lighting and shading design | Smart Lighting, Smart Blinds |
| Outdoor living | Outdoor Entertainment |
| Florida-specific demands | Surveillance, Outdoor Entertainment |
| The network underneath | Wi-Fi (Cisco, Araknis, Ubiquiti, CommScope) |
| Future-proofing with open walls | Automation |
| Simplicity as the goal | Multi Room Audio, Automation |

Every thesis section has a real Husky service behind it. That is what keeps the page
honest.

## Copy register

From the reference, worth adopting:

- Second person, direct. "If you're building a new home in Florida, don't wait."
- Short paragraphs — often a single sentence.
- Concrete over superlative.
- Each section makes one point and stops.
- Closes by naming the service area.

## Open items — need the client

Still unresolved from the content inventory, and they limit how strong the pages can be:

| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Founding year | A specific year beats "20+ years" |
| Certification names / dealer status | "Certified" is asserted with no detail |
| Brand logo assets | Without them, partner proof stays text-only |
| Project photos | The single biggest upgrade available to both pages |
| Service area list | The reference article closes on this; Husky has only "South Florida" |
| Contact form — yes or no | Legacy site has none; WhatsApp is the primary channel |

## Order of work

1. Home landing page
2. Philosophy page

Not in parallel. The home page establishes the visual language and the section
vocabulary that the second page reuses.
